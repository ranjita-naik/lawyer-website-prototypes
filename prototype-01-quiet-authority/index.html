Areas of practice

Five areas, one practice.

The practice is deliberately general within a narrow band: disputes, and the contracts and decisions that give rise to them. What connects the five is a preference for understanding a matter completely before advising on it.

Constitutional and Administrative Matters

Representing clients in matters involving government authorities, regulatory decisions and constitutional remedies before the High Court. Much of the work lies in reading the record closely, and in understanding how a decision was arrived at before it is questioned.

Read more on this area

Commercial Disputes

Advising and representing businesses in disputes arising from contracts, transactions and commercial relationships. Where a matter can be resolved efficiently and on reasonable terms, that route is considered first.

Arbitration and Mediation

Acting as counsel in institutional and ad-hoc arbitration proceedings, and advising parties considering mediation as a route to resolving disputes outside of court. Which forum suits a dispute is a question worth settling early.

Civil Litigation

Representing individuals and businesses in civil proceedings before trial and appellate courts, including matters relating to contracts, property and civil wrongs. Civil litigation rewards preparation more reliably than it rewards speed.

Real Estate and Property Disputes

Advising on property transactions and representing clients in disputes concerning ownership, title, tenancy and partition of property. Title questions are often older than the dispute itself, and are treated accordingly.

On method

Most of a matter is settled long before it is argued.

In how carefully the papers are read. In how honestly the risk is described at the outset. In telling a client early what a dispute is likely to cost in time as well as money, and what it is realistically capable of achieving. The work is unhurried by design; the hurry, where it is needed, is kept for the days it actually helps.
