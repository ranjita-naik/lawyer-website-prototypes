Areas of practice

Four areas of work, and what each one usually means in practice.

The formal name of an area of law is rarely how anyone describes their own problem. Each area below is set out twice: once in the language of the courts, and once in the language people actually use when they call.

  • Family and Succession Matters

    When a family has to settle what it has held together.

    Advice for individuals and families on succession, inheritance and the disputes that follow from them — handled with care for how personal these matters usually are.

    • Wills, probate and letters of administration
    • Intestate succession and legal heirship
    • Partition of ancestral property
    • Family settlements and their documentation

    Read more about this area

  • Civil Litigation

    When a disagreement has to be decided by a court.

    Representation for individuals and businesses in civil proceedings before trial and appellate courts, including matters relating to contracts, property and civil wrongs.

    • Suits and written statements
    • Interim applications and injunctions
    • Recovery and contractual claims
    • Appeals and revisions

  • Arbitration and Mediation

    When a dispute might be settled without a courtroom.

    Acting as counsel in institutional and ad-hoc arbitration proceedings, and advising parties who are considering mediation as a route to resolving a dispute outside of court.

    Mediation is raised at the beginning of a matter wherever it is a realistic option, and particularly where the parties have a relationship that will outlast the dispute. It is not suitable in every case, and where it is not, that is said plainly rather than attempted first and abandoned later.

    • Institutional and ad-hoc arbitration
    • Mediation, and preparing for it
    • Negotiated settlements and their recording
    • Advice on arbitration and dispute clauses

  • Real Estate and Property Disputes

    When ownership of a property is unclear or contested.

    Advice on property transactions, and representation in disputes concerning ownership, title, tenancy and the partition of property.

    • Title examination and documentation
    • Tenancy and possession disputes
    • Partition and co-ownership
    • Development and society-related matters

Not sure which of these describes your situation?

Most matters do not arrive neatly labelled. Describe what has happened in your own words and the office will tell you whether it is something it can help with.
