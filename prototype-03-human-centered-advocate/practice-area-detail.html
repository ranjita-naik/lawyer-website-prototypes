Family and Succession Matters

When a family has to settle what it has held together.

Advice for individuals and families on succession, inheritance and the disputes that follow from them — handled with care for how personal these matters usually are.

At a glance

Who this is usually for
Individuals, siblings, spouses and families holding property in common.
Where matters are heard
Bombay High Court and the City Civil Court, Mumbai; some matters settle in mediation.
Languages
English, Hindi, Marathi.

What these matters usually involve

Succession disputes rarely begin as disputes. They begin with a death in the family, a property that nobody has formally divided, a will that cannot be found, or an arrangement that everyone once understood and no longer agrees on. By the time an advocate is consulted, the legal question is often smaller than the history surrounding it.

The first task is usually documentary rather than adversarial: establishing who the legal heirs are, what the deceased actually owned, whether a valid will exists, and what the records at the sub-registrar and the society or revenue office show. A good deal of what looks like a dispute turns out to be an incomplete paper trail.

Where the work goes from there

Once the position is clear, there are broadly three routes. A family settlement recorded properly and registered, which is often the quickest and least damaging. A mediated resolution, where the parties are willing to talk but need structure and a neutral third person to do it. Or a suit — for partition, for probate or letters of administration, or for a declaration of title — where agreement is not available.

The route is chosen with you, not for you. What is set out in the first conversation is what each option is likely to involve: the steps, the documents you would need to gather, the approximate stages, and the effect each route tends to have on relationships within the family.

Matters commonly handled

  • Wills, probate and letters of administration
  • Intestate succession and establishing legal heirship
  • Partition of ancestral and jointly held property
  • Family settlements, and recording them so they hold
  • Disputes over transfer of flats, shares and society records after a death

The information on this page is general. Succession is governed by different statutes depending on the personal law that applies, and the correct approach in any particular matter depends entirely on its own facts.
