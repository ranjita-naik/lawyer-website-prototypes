The office

One advocate, from the first conversation to the last.

This is an independent practice, not a large firm. There is no team of associates between you and the person handling your matter, which means the advocate you speak to at the first meeting is the one who reads the papers, drafts the pleadings and appears in court.

Much of the work that reaches these chambers is not purely commercial. Property, succession and family disputes carry long histories, and often involve people who will still have to speak to one another afterwards. Where a matter can be settled by agreement, that is said early and plainly. Where it cannot, it is prepared properly.

What to expect

What happens after you contact the office.

These three steps are the same in every matter, so that nothing about the process itself comes as a surprise.

  1. 1

    You get in touch

    Write to the office with a short description of the matter and any dates that already apply to it. A reply will say whether this is something the chambers can help with, and if not, that is said straight away.

  2. 2

    A first conversation

    By appointment at the Fort chambers, or by telephone. The purpose is to understand the facts, the documents that exist, and what you would like to happen. Bring whatever papers you have; they need not be in order.

  3. 3

    A plain explanation of the options

    Where a matter could be resolved by negotiation or mediation, that is set out alongside what litigation would involve — the likely stages, the time and the work required. The decision is then yours to make.
